How to use this review

10 minutes, one read. Seven blocks, one per Midterm 2 topic. Each gives you the idea, the key terms, a case with numbers and a sentence to use in your answer. For full explanations, quizzes and flashcards open the complete guide.

The idea in one line

Every answer: claim in two lines → concept in your own words → real case with facts → limit and recommendation for the firm.

1. The pipeline: the model is only one link

  • Pipeline: collection → preparation → training → evaluation → deployment with monitoring. It is not a straight line: if evaluation disappoints, you go back to the data.
  • CRISP-DM: six phases (business, data, preparation, modelling, evaluation, deployment).
  • Features = input clues. Label = the known answer. Feature engineering = turning raw data into a useful clue (from "booking date" and "dinner date" to "days in advance").
  • Data leakage: using information you would not have at prediction time, such as the cancellation date to predict a cancellation. The score looks great and is false.
  • Baseline: the simple rule to beat. Honest split: train on the past, test on the period after.

In your answer: "I start from the decision to change, not from the technology."

2. Evaluating a model: accuracy can lie

Formula

Precision = TP / (TP + FP): how many alarms are real. Recall = TP / (TP + FN): how many real cases you find. F1 = 2·P·R / (P + R).

Worked example · Restaurant cancellations (course example)

100 bookings: TP 15, FP 10, FN 5, TN 70. Accuracy 85%, precision 15/25 = 60%, recall 15/20 = 75%, F1 ≈ 66.7%. The lazy model "nobody cancels" scores 80% accuracy and 0% recall.

  • Choose the metric by the cost of the error: missing a case is expensive (fraud, churn) → recall; every alarm triggers a costly action (technician, discount) → precision.
  • Overfitting: 2% error on training, 25% on validation. The test set is opened once, at the end.
  • Model drift: the world changes and the model gets worse.

Real case · Zillow Offers, 2021

The pricing algorithm used to buy homes overvalued many of them in an unstable market. On 2 November 2021 the business was shut down: a $304m inventory write-down in the quarter and about 25% of staff cut.

20 Oct review: a decision tree is readable but unstable; k-means groups data without labels and must be re-run from several starting points on scaled data.

3. From model to team: what counts is the changed decision

  • The CEO with "dashboards everywhere" understands nothing: every analysis needs a question, an owner, an action.
  • Hub-and-spoke: a central hub (platform, shared definitions, quality) and analysts inside the business units. It keeps both consistency and closeness.
  • RACI: for each decision, who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.
  • Pick the first project with a score: value 40%, feasibility 25%, data readiness 20%, adoption 15%. In the course case (a fictional firm) the profitability dashboard (4.40) beats churn (3.35).
  • Deliver small and measure value against a control group.

In your answer: "An analytics team is judged by the decisions it changes, not by the reports it produces."

4. IoT: where should the machine think?

  • Chain: sensor → data → decision → actuator. Data is worth something only if it triggers an action.
  • Edge = on the device (instant response). Fog = site level (aggregates, coordinates, buffers). Cloud = history and model training.
  • Compute goes where the deadline needs it. Rejecting a bottle in 40 ms when a cloud round trip takes 120 ms → edge.
  • Store-and-forward: if the network drops, the local node keeps the events and sends them later.

Real case · Mirai, 2016

Cameras and video recorders with factory passwords became a botnet. On 21 October 2016 the attack on Dyn (DNS) took many major sites offline for hours. Every device needs an identity, protected access and signed updates with rollback.

Formula · Payback

Payback = investment / net annual benefit. Turbines (hypothetical): €300k / €180k ≈ 1.7 years. If avoided losses halve → 5 years.

In your answer: "A pilot deserves investment if it links a timely local action to a result measured against a baseline."

5. Text becomes data: the NLP pipeline

  • Text is the richest and messiest data a firm owns (unstructured data).
  • Seven steps: segmentation, tokenisation, stop words, stemming, lemmatisation, POS tagging, NER.
  • "He goes to college." = 5 tokens. Stemming cuts endings ("studies" → "studi"); lemmatisation uses the dictionary (am, are, is → be).
  • NER recognises entities: "Ticketmaster" → organisation, "£65" → money.

Watch out

A standard stop-word list also removes "not": "The show was not good" becomes "show good". For sentiment, keep it.

In your answer: "Every cleaning step is a choice: I state it and explain why."

6. From sentiment to a decision

  • Sentiment analysis: positive, negative, neutral. Aspect-based: the tone on each aspect ("great boss fight, slow matchmaking").
  • Emotions (Plutchik's wheel, 8 primary): softmax when classes exclude each other (probabilities sum to 1); sigmoid when you need several labels at once.
  • RoBERTa on GoEmotions: average F1 0.45, "gratitude" above 0.9. Domain shift: on different texts the model gets worse.
  • Four traps: frequency ≠ impact, sentiment ≠ behaviour, sample ≠ reality, correlation ≠ cause.

Real case · Twitter mood, 2011

The "calm" mood of almost 10 million tweets predicted the direction of the Dow Jones with 87.6% accuracy. But the test covered about 20 trading days: a correlation, not a cause.

In your answer: "A score is a signal. Before spending, I ask which evidence would change the decision."

7. AI in processes: automate a process, not a step

  • Hyperautomation: automating whole processes, not a single step. Four levels: see (process mining) → predict → prescribe → adapt.
  • Process mining: from the event log (case ID, activity, timestamp) it rebuilds the real process. Siemens found 923,000 variants of one process (vendor-reported).
  • Uplift: predicting who is at risk is not enough; you need to know whether the intervention changes anything. Sleeping dogs = customers the intervention harms.

Real case · UWV, the Netherlands, 2017

The model found risky cases well, but the emails sent to them did not help: 3.8% errors with the system versus 4.0% without, not a significant difference.

Formula · Value of an intervention

V = p × u × C − k. With p 0.4, u 0.25, C €2,000, k €50 → V = €150. If u = 0, V = −€50.

  • Framed autonomy: an AI agent acts inside a frame set by people (e.g. refunds up to €100; above that, a human decides).

Real case · Air Canada, 2024

The chatbot gave a wrong bereavement-fare rule. The tribunal ordered the airline to pay C$812.02: the agent acts, the company answers for it.

Before you walk in

  • Every answer has a case with numbers and a limit.
  • Name the metric and the why (which error costs more).
  • Prediction ≠ cause, score ≠ decision, step ≠ process.
  • Always close with a concrete recommendation and how to measure it.
